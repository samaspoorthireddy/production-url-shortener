# Module 4 Micro-Exercise: Blameless Postmortems

## 1. Systemic Focus vs. Personal Blame
When a mistake happens, asking "why did you do that?" immediately triggers defensiveness and fear of reprisal, leading to rationalization or cover-ups. Conversely, asking "why did the system allow that?" shifts the focus from personal fault to collective problem-solving. This reframing treats the person as an investigator of systemic weaknesses rather than a target of blame, encouraging transparency and productive analysis.

## 2. Reporting Incidents Under Punitive Policies
If reporting a self-introduced bug guarantees punishment, the rational incentive is to conceal it and hope it is never discovered or gets blamed on another factor. If the environment is blameless, reporting it immediately is the obvious choice. Surfacing issues early prevents compounding system failures and lowers the overall recovery cost.
